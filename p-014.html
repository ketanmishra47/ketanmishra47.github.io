P—014 Campaign 2026

Miduty Liver Detox campaign.

A full campaign for a ₹1,400 supplement, built on one insight: clinics charge ₹50,000 for glutathione, and your liver makes it for free. Strategy, avatars, a quiz landing page, 5 emails, a reel and a static ad.

Built forMiduty · Liver Detox
FormatStrategy + full funnel
StatusContract campaign · July 2026

// Context

Miduty sells Liver Detox, a milk thistle and NAC supplement, at ₹1,400 a bottle. Miduty brought me in on contract to build a campaign on the pigmentation angle: get qualified leads and turn them into subscribers.

The product takes months to show anything. A campaign built for first purchase sells a bottle to someone who quits in week 3 and asks for a refund.

So I built the whole campaign around the subscription. The people worth finding are the ones who will stay for 4 months.

// The insight

Ask any skin clinic in urban India about pigmentation and glutathione comes up within a minute. Drips cost ₹8,000 to ₹12,000 a sitting, and the clinic recommends 5.

Nobody mentions that the liver makes glutathione every day, for free, and falls behind when it's overloaded. There's no margin in saying so.

That gap carries the campaign. It takes a word she already knows and already pays for, and shows her she gets it free.

// The funnel

The reel and the paid ad earn the click. A 60-second quiz earns the email and tags her by what is most likely driving her pigmentation.

The 5 emails do the selling. The tag decides which version of Email 2 she gets, so each avatar hears the mechanism that fits her life.

Anyone already convinced gets a shortcut. The remarketing static skips the quiz and goes straight to the product page.

// 5 avatars, each one a quiz answer

Urban women, 26 to 38, who have already spent money on their pigmentation. Each avatar maps to a tag the quiz assigns, which makes them a targeting system you can run.

The one we don't sell to. One avatar is nursing. Miduty's own product page tells nursing users to check with a doctor first, so her emails teach and stop at Email 3 with "talk to your doctor." She never sees an offer.

That exclusion sits in the ad account as well as the email tool, so the "Shop now" static never reaches her either.

// Landing page + quiz

Miduty's product page already converts warm traffic. The landing page has one job: earn the email and assign the tag.

6 questions in Hinglish, a score on screen, and the full breakdown by email. A Shop button sits beside the product for anyone ready to buy.

// The quiz on a phone

// 5 emails

One job per email. Email 1 explains the score. Email 2 branches 5 ways on the tag. Email 3 introduces the product, Email 4 brings the proof and Email 5 makes the subscription offer.

A language rule. Feelings go in Hindi. Body parts and science go in English. The ad, the page and the inbox all sound like the same person.

Honest about time. Every touchpoint says the product takes months. That costs some first purchases, and it keeps the buyers who will stay.

// The reel

Founder-led and organic. It teaches the link between the liver and pigmentation, then asks viewers to comment "LIVER" for the full breakdown.

Built with AI video from a single still of the founder. The voiceover and the mechanism graphics went in at the edit.

// How it gets measured

3 hooks over 1 shared body. Any difference in results comes from the hook alone.

Cost per qualified lead is the north star. A qualified lead is a quiz completion with a tag. An untagged lead can't get the right Email 2, so it's worth a fraction of a tagged one.

Refund rate and month-3 churn are the guardrails. If either climbs, the campaign is over-promising.

// Outcome

The full campaign shipped to Miduty as one system: strategy, avatars, landing page, emails and creative, all tied to the quiz tag.

It shows how I build a campaign: 1 insight, carried from the first reel to the last email, with the guardrails written in before launch.

₹50,000 at the clinic. ₹0 from your liver. 1 idea, 5 emails.
