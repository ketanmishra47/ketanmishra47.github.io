The AI Creative Lab #02: The most expensive meeting in your creative team

Let me guess how your mornings go. The data-mining meeting I ran for too long, what I learnt, and the daily pull, morning view and 5 testing stages that replaced it. Plus a Claude prompt for yesterday's Meta export.

Length859 words · 4 min read

The AI Creative Lab · Issue 02

Subject: The most expensive meeting in your creative team
Preview: Let me guess how your mornings go.

Let me guess how your mornings go

Your strategists and media buyers sit down together before anyone writes a brief. The plan is simple: look at what's working and decide what to make next.

Then someone shares their screen and opens Ads Manager.

One campaign at a time, one ad set at a time, the numbers come out. Everyone waits while the buyer filters and scrolls. Somebody asks for last week's figure to compare. Somebody else reads the same ROI and draws the opposite conclusion.

The strategists can't brief until a buyer surfaces the data. The buyers, who should be managing spend, are doing an analyst's job. By the time everyone agrees on what won yesterday, a good part of the morning has gone, and the briefs haven't started.

Tomorrow you'll do it again.

Sound familiar?

If it does, you're in good company. I ran that meeting for a long time.

My team has shipped 14,513 ad creatives across 5 performance verticals: personal loans, debt relief, Medicare, ACA and auto insurance. We ship 200+ a day now. Our mornings used to look exactly like the one above: tens of campaigns dug through by hand, numbers pulled live, arguments about what they meant, hours gone.

That meeting cost us the hours we needed to act on what it found.

What I learnt

The meeting existed for one reason: strategists couldn't see performance on their own. Every brief depended on someone else opening an ad account first.

Strategists needed their own view of yesterday's numbers, ready before they sat down. Once they had it, the data-mining session ended and the morning went back to briefing.

How to put it in place

1. Pull the data once, at the same time every day

We built a dashboard on the Meta API. A scheduled job runs at 1:30 PM IST, when the US day has closed, and pulls the previous day's campaign and ad-set data into one sortable table.

Pick the time your market's day ends. After that, nobody pulls numbers by hand.

2. Show only the numbers that change a brief

  • Hook rate and hold rate. Is the opening stopping people, and is the body keeping them?
  • ROI, CTR and CPC. Is the attention turning into money?
  • The top 10 creatives by ROI. The list the day's briefs come from.
  • Spend and gross margin by team. So leadership reads the same page as the strategists.

Anything that doesn't change what you brief next goes in a second tab, or nowhere.

3. Give every creative a stage

StageWhat it means
TestedLaunched and spending its test budget
SandboxEarly signal, needs more spend to judge
ValidationHolding up at higher spend
WinnerProven. New briefs come from here
ScaledTaking real budget across accounts

Each asset shows its spend, gross margin and revenue next to its stage. Set your own thresholds for moving up, write them down and use the same ones for every vertical.

4. Ask a different question each morning

Ask "what moved stage, and what do we make from it?" A strategist opens the dashboard, sorts the table and briefs the day's variants. Media buyers keep their mornings for spend.

Start tomorrow morning

You can test this before building anything. Export yesterday's ad-level data from Ads Manager as a CSV, with spend, impressions, 3-second views, ThruPlays, clicks, purchases and revenue. Give it to Claude with this prompt:

This is yesterday's ad-level export from Meta Ads Manager.

1. Calculate for every ad:
   hook rate = 3-second views / impressions
   hold rate = ThruPlays / 3-second views
   CTR, CPC and ROI (revenue minus spend, divided by spend)

2. Give me the top 10 ads by ROI, with hook rate and hold rate beside each.

3. Sort every ad into one stage using my thresholds:
   Tested / Sandbox / Validation / Winner / Scaled
   [write your spend and ROI thresholds for each stage here]

4. List the ads that moved up a stage compared with the export
   I gave you yesterday, if I gave you one.

5. For each Winner, suggest 2 variant briefs: one that keeps the hook
   and changes the body, one that keeps the body and changes the hook.

Show the numbers in tables. Don't recommend pausing anything; I decide that.

Send the output to your strategists before the meeting, and run it at the same time every day for a week. If they stop asking buyers for numbers, you know it's worth building properly.
