W—009 Playbook

How to ship 200+ ad creatives a day without growing the team

The operating system behind 200+ ad creatives a day across 5 verticals: one daily performance view, briefs from winners, automated handoffs and AI first drafts.

Length1,493 words · 7 min read

Our creative org ships 200+ ad creatives a day across 5 performance verticals: personal loans, debt relief, Medicare, ACA and auto insurance. The count since we started tracking is 14,513.

The team didn't grow to match that number. What changed was the operating system around it: how we read performance, how we brief, how work moves between people, and where AI does the first draft.

This playbook is that system, written down. It's for creative leads, heads of growth and founders running paid social who keep hearing "we need more creatives" and can't hire their way out of it.

The short version. Read performance once a day in one place. Brief only from winners. Let software move the work between people. Give AI the first draft and keep humans on the angle and the claims.

Part 1

Read the numbers before anyone briefs

Shipping more creatives doesn't help if nobody can tell which ones are working. So the first thing to fix is how the team reads performance.

Our mornings used to start with a huddle between creative strategists and media buyers. It was meant to be a decisions meeting. It became a data-mining session: tens of campaigns and ad sets dug through by hand, numbers pulled live, arguments about what they meant. Strategists waited on buyers. Buyers did analyst work. Hours went every morning.

We replaced the huddle with a dashboard that pulls from the Meta API once a day. The pull fires at 1:30 PM IST, when the US day has closed, and lands the previous day's campaign and ad-set data in one sortable table.

What goes on the morning view

Keep it to the numbers that change what you brief next:

  • Hook rate and hold rate, to see whether the first 3 seconds and the body are doing their jobs.
  • ROI, CTR and CPC, to see whether the attention turns into money.
  • Top 10 creatives by ROI, the list strategists brief from.
  • Spend and gross margin by team, so leadership reads the same page as everyone else.

Give every creative a stage

Every asset sits in one of 5 stages, with its spend, gross margin and revenue next to it:

StageWhat it means
TestedLaunched and spending its test budget
SandboxEarly signal, needs more spend to judge
ValidationHolding up at higher spend
WinnerProven. This is what the next briefs come from
ScaledTaking real budget across accounts

Once the stages exist, the morning question changes from "what happened yesterday?" to "what moved stage, and what do we make from it?" Strategists open the dashboard, sort the table and brief the day's variants.

Part 2

Brief from winners, every time

At 200+ a day, nobody writes 200 ideas from scratch. Most of the volume is structured variation on things that already work. That only holds if winners are easy to find and easy to take apart.

One taxonomy for every vertical

Our 5 verticals used to run in silos, each with its own strategist and its own hook library. A hook that worked in personal loans never reached auto insurance. Performance compounded inside each vertical and stopped there.

The fix was a shared language. Every vertical names hooks, body structures, CTA patterns and fatigue curves the same way. A winning hook in one vertical gets named and indexed in a shared library, and a strategist in another vertical can pull it, see the pattern and adapt it to their audience.

We share across verticals on a weekly rhythm: what's working this week and what's worth adapting. In practice a hook that wins in Medicare gets adapted to ACA the same week.

Break every winning script down the same way

Two analysts reading the same winning script used to describe it differently, so the "what works" library was noisy. Now every script is broken into the same 6 structural elements:

  1. Pure claim
  2. Pure proof
  3. Bonding and relationship building
  4. Setup for a later payoff
  5. Objection handling
  6. Storytelling

On top of that we tag the hook type, the psychological trigger, the archetype and anything that needs a compliance flag. When every winner is described in the same fields, you can rank hooks by how often they win and write the next variant against the pattern.

Make "spin a winner" one click

In our internal tool, Creo, a strategist can create a variation job straight from a winning creative. The job inherits the parent angle and its performance, and the parent script sits next to the draft. The strategist changes what needs to change and keeps the working parts.

Every angle keeps its family tree. One debt relief angle thread, DEB089, shows 14 creatives, 3 winners, 5 money losers and $48.2k in total spend. That record tells a strategist when an angle still has room and when it's done.

Part 3

Let software move the work

Before automation, 2 things ate our team. Handoffs broke daily: scripts sat in inboxes, approvals never pinged and status never got back to the writer. And scale broke everything else: thousands of files with no shared naming or location, so reusing a winner meant a 20-minute search.

One workflow, many branches

We run the whole creative pipeline on ClickUp, with one master n8n workflow on top. It has 30+ branches. Each one fires on a specific ClickUp event and does one job:

  • assigning a video editor
  • updating custom fields
  • processing attachments
  • routing validation and enforcing names

When a creative is delivered, it posts to the task as a comment with the file link and is assigned to whoever owns the next step. Nobody has to send a Slack message to move work forward. The workflow handles 100+ creative handoffs a day.

Name every file so you can find it in a year

Every creative gets a structured filename: date, vertical, offer, variant. Combined with status groups by quarter (WINNER Q3'24, WINNER Q4'24), anyone can pull a winning asset from any quarter in seconds. Winners get reused because nobody has to search for them.

Part 4

Shape the team around the flow

The structure that runs this:

  • 5 creative strategists, 1 per vertical. Each owns their vertical's hook library and knows its audience and its compliance rules.
  • 5 video editors in one shared pool. Any editor can pick up a brief from any vertical, from a queue sorted by deadline.
  • 1 media buying lead over the buyers across all verticals, sending requests into the same inbox the strategists work from.
  • A leadership view showing jobs in flight, winners this week, money losers, spend and average ROAS for every vertical on one screen.

Strategists stay deep in one vertical. Editors stay vertical-agnostic, so no vertical waits on "its" editor. Each vertical ships daily, and the weekly cross-vertical share is where the org learns from itself.

Part 5

Where AI does the work

The jump to 200+ a day came after we put AI into copy and video production. Here's where it sits.

Analysis

We built an agentic workflow that runs the 6-element breakdown on winning creatives. Claude reads the scripts. Gemini reads the video frames. Both work from one system-instructions file that defines the analysis schema, so the output has the same structure every time.

The output is what a strategist would build by hand on a good day: an analysis table per file, a hook taxonomy ranked by how often each hook appears, structural timing, compliance flags on every script line, and a one-page blueprint of the batch.

First drafts

From that blueprint, the pipeline writes replica scripts ready to shoot, time-coded by structural element: hook, proof, mechanism, objection, CTA. The next phase turns those scripts into full talking-head sequences with B-roll.

What stays with people

The strategist picks the angle, reads the dashboard and decides what deserves a variant. Compliance in health, lending and insurance stays with a human who knows the vertical. The AI writes the first draft against a pattern the team has already proven.

Two analysts running the pipeline on the same batch now get the same answer. Over the same period, profit on our performance campaigns went up 30%.

Start this week

4 things to set up first

You don't need all of this on day 1. This is the order I'd build it in again:

  1. One daily performance view. Pull yesterday's data at the same time every day and put hook rate, hold rate, ROI and CTR in one sortable table. Cancel the meeting it replaces.
  2. Stages and names. Give every creative a stage and a structured filename. Group winners by quarter.
  3. A shared taxonomy. Agree on the 6 structural elements and your hook types, then tag every winner with them.
  4. Automate the most-broken handoff. Find the step where work most often waits for a ping and build that branch first. Add branches one at a time.

AI goes in after these 4, once there's a clean library of tagged winners for it to learn from.
