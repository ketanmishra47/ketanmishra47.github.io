Coding Sharks gesture FX.

A brand demo you interact with using your hands. Particles assemble the brand. Your gestures scatter them.

Built for Coding Sharks · Indore
Format Single-file gesture demo
Status Shipped 2025

// Context

Coding Sharks is a coding institute in Bhawarkua, Indore. 5,000+ active students, 4.9★ Google rating, alumni placed at Accenture and CSI. The brand had every credential a hiring student looks for — and the same feed presence as any other institute.

Static promos got scrolled past. Reels explaining the curriculum landed on the same flat dropoff curve. The brand needed a moment a student would stop on, send to friends, and walk into the institute already curious about.

The brief: a brand demo that lives outside a Reel. Something that uses the camera the student already has. Something that doesn't ask them to install an app.

// Approach

Single-file HTML. No build step, no framework, no installer. One link to run on any device with a camera and a modern browser.

The interaction: 1,800 particles assemble into the brand's words — SHARKS, CODE, HIRED, BUILD, INDORE, AI. Raise your hands and the particles scatter around your fingertips like a force field. Drop your hands and they reassemble. The words rotate every 4 seconds when no one's interacting, so the demo never goes idle.

MediaPipe Hands handles gesture tracking. HTML5 Canvas handles the particle system. Hand landmarks act as repulsion vectors against each particle. Particles carry noise-driven drift so they never feel rigid.

Two engineering choices that mattered most for cross-device reliability:

  • Manual frame pump for MediaPipe instead of its built-in Camera utility. The utility hangs on iOS Safari mid-session, the manual pump survives.
  • No ctx.filter blur calls anywhere. Safari renders them inconsistently. All glow effects come from layered fill passes with stacked alpha.

Particle count capped at 1,800. Past that, older phones running Safari start choking on the per-frame draw loop.

// Live demo

Allow camera permission in the frame to interact. Move your hands. Use the buttons in the demo to switch the assembly word.

// Outcome

The brand became something a student could touch. The demo lives on the institute's website as the hero element. It runs in the background of Reels and stories. Walk-in students pull out their phones at the front desk and try it themselves.

A coding institute with a brand toy. The shortest path from feed to walk-in.
